What is Ausbildung?

A paid, 2–3.5 year qualification — Germany's own skills engine

Ausbildung (dual vocational training) is the system that trains roughly half of Germany's workforce. A trainee signs a training contract with a company, spends 3–4 days a week in the company and 1–2 days in a state vocational school, and receives a monthly training allowance (Ausbildungsvergütung) for the entire duration instead of paying fees. The qualification is nationally recognised, ends with a chamber examination (IHK/HWK or health-sector examination) and is treated as a full professional qualification — not a "second-best" option.

  • No tuition fees at public vocational schools — you are an employee, not a fee-paying student.
  • Paid from day one — the legal minimum for a contract starting in 2026 is €724 gross in year 1, rising each year.
  • Rises every year — typical year-on-year increases under collective agreements run from about 5% to 10%.
  • Recognised qualification plus a residence title that can lead to a settlement permit after 2 years of skilled work.
  • Real shortage occupations — healthcare, IT, skilled trades and finance are on Germany's official bottleneck lists.

Sources: Federal Institute for Vocational Education and Training (BIBB) minimum training pay figures as reported for 2026 start contracts; Make it in Germany (Federal Government portal); acaluna.com (BIBB / Federal Employment Agency Entgeltatlas compilation, reviewed Sept 2026).

Financial Proof

The blocked account (Sperrkonto), explained

A blocked account is a German bank account into which you deposit money that is then released to you in monthly instalments after you arrive. It is the standard way to prove to the German authorities that you can cover your living costs when your own income does not yet cover them.

  • €992 per month — the official standard amount, unchanged since the winter semester 2024 and confirmed for 2026.
  • €11,904 for a 12-month visa — the figure embassies expect on the certificate.
  • Not always necessary — if your training contract pays at least €1,048 gross / €822 net per month, the contract itself is your proof of self-sufficiency.
  • Mixed proof is allowed — a lower-paid contract can be topped up with a blocked account or a declaration of commitment (Verpflichtungserklärung) from a person in Germany.
  • Always check your embassy — the visa office handling your file may require a different amount depending on the visa type and any scholarship funding.

Source: Expatrio blocked-amount guide (€992/month, €11,904/year, tables for 2022–2026) and acaluna.com citing Make it in Germany for the €1,048 gross / €822 net visa threshold.

Step-wise Process Flowchart

India → Germany: the complete Ausbildung pathway

Five stages apply to every candidate. After the German language stage the route splits into two legal paths: Path A — finish A2 and enter with a blocked account, or Path B — finish B2 and enter without a blocked account because your training pay already proves self-sufficiency.

Stages 1–5 · common to both paths

1
Profile & eligibility check

School-leaving certificate (10+2 or equivalent), age, health, passport. No legal age limit for vocational training.

2
German language — A1 to B2

Online or offline classes in India. A1→A2→B1 are built step by step; B2 is the healthcare standard.

3
Documents & recognition

CV in German, transcripts, translations, Zeugnisbewertung / equivalence check of your school diploma.

4
Apply & interview

Training-place search, applications, video interviews with German employers, trial internship (Praktikum) where possible.

5
Sign the training contract

Berufsausbildungsvertrag with a fixed monthly training allowance, start date and training occupation.

Then the route splits ↓

Path A · A2 route

Enter with a blocked account

For candidates who complete level A2 (and continue to B1/B2 in Germany) before or during their placement.

A1

Finish A2 German in India — you may continue to B1/B2 in Germany alongside the training.

A2

Open a blocked account (Sperrkonto) and deposit €11,904 (€992 × 12 months) — the 2026 standard amount.

A3

Apply for the visa for vocational training (§16a AufenthG) at the German mission in India: contract + blocked account certificate + language certificate.

A4

Pay the €75 national visa fee, attend the appointment, submit biometrics and documents.

A5

Arrive and register — Anmeldung at the town hall, residence permit at the immigration office, bank account, health insurance.

Why this route: it lets you start earlier, but you must show the full blocked amount because your contract alone does not yet meet the self-sufficiency threshold of €1,048 gross / €822 net per month.

Path B · B2 route

Enter without a blocked account

For candidates who complete B2 in India before applying — the faster, cheaper legal route.

B1

Complete B2 German in India — the level healthcare roles require and the strongest signal to employers.

B2

Secure a training contract paying at least €1,048 gross / €822 net per month — the contract then serves as your proof of self-sufficiency, so no blocked account is needed.

B3

Apply for the §16a vocational training visa with the signed contract and your B2 certificate.

B4

Pay the €75 visa fee and attend the appointment — no €11,904 deposit to arrange.

B5

Arrive and register — Anmeldung, residence permit, bank account, health insurance, vocational school enrolment.

Why this route: no blocked-account cash requirement, faster visa file, and you start the training already able to work independently with patients and customers.

Frequently Asked

Straight answers before you commit

Is a blocked account mandatory for an Ausbildung visa?

No. If your training contract pays at least €1,048 gross / €822 net per month, the contract itself is your proof of self-sufficiency. If it pays less, the difference must be covered — typically with a blocked account of €11,904 (€992 × 12 months) or a declaration of commitment from someone in Germany.

How much German do I need?

B1 is the practical minimum for many Ausbildung roles; healthcare and nursing roles such as caregiving and physiotherapy generally require B2 because real patient interaction demands it. We teach A1 through B2 in India, online and offline.

Do I pay tuition fees?

At public vocational schools, no — tuition is €0, and in a dual Ausbildung you are an employee receiving a monthly training allowance. Physiotherapy is the exception: it is school-based and fee structures vary, so we screen only programmes that pay an allowance.

Is there an age limit?

No legal age limit applies to vocational training in Germany. Employers assess school qualifications, language level, motivation and health suitability.

Can I bring my spouse or family?

Family reunification rules depend on your residence title, your income and available housing. Because Ausbildung pay is modest in the early years, reunification is usually more realistic once you are working as a qualified skilled worker. Ask us to assess your specific case.

Can I work a side job during training?

With a residence permit for vocational training you may take a second job of up to 20 hours per week, as long as it is separate from your training. In practice the dual week plus vocational school makes this difficult, so treat it as a top-up rather than a plan.

What happens after I finish?

You qualify as a skilled worker, your pay typically more than doubles the training allowance, and your residence title changes to one for qualified employment. After completing the Ausbildung and working in Germany for two years, you can apply for a settlement permit if the other conditions are met.

Why do you show ranges instead of one salary figure?

Because the allowance is fixed individually in your contract and depends on the employer, whether a collective agreement applies, the region and your training year. Anyone quoting a single guaranteed figure for every candidate is not describing the German system accurately.
